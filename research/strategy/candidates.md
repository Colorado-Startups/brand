# Strategy candidates and scores

S1 Strategy, 2026-11-02. This file records how the mission, vision and tagline in `data/strategy.yaml` were chosen, and every candidate that lost. If this file and `data/strategy.yaml` disagree, the YAML wins; fix this file in the same pull request.

## The rubric

Five criteria, each scored 0, 1 or 2, for 10 points at most. The thresholds were written before any candidate was scored.

| Criterion | 2 | 1 | 0 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Founders-first | Founders are named and are the subject or the first beneficiary | Founders implied (entrepreneurs, builders, startups) or named second | Founders absent |
| Colorado-specific | Names Colorado as the place the work happens | Implies Colorado (Front Range, "here") | No Colorado |
| Short | Tagline 8 words or fewer; mission 15 or fewer; vision 12 or fewer (goals set here) | Tagline 9 to 12; mission 16 to 25; vision 13 to 20 | Longer |
| True today | Mission and tagline: every claim shows on the live site or in a public record. Vision: the direction already shows in something the community does, and it claims nothing has arrived | Mission and tagline: one claim is aspirational. Vision: no proof of direction yet, or it overclaims | A claim is false today |
| Distinct from peers | No peer on the landscape map uses the same core claim or phrase, and no one holds it as a trademark | Shares a core claim or key phrase with a peer | A peer or a trademark holder already owns the line |

**Peers** are the organizations on the landscape map in `data/strategy.yaml`, plus any founder community a Colorado founder can join from here.

**Ties.** The line already public wins; then the shorter line.

**Word counts** were computed here with a whitespace split (hyphenated words count once).

## How the scores were made

1. **Author pass.** S1 scored every candidate against the rubric and the evidence below.
2. **Blind review, round 1.** Three reviewer agents scored the first nine candidates from a shared evidence pack. Each saw the candidates under arbitrary labels in a different order, and none saw the author's scores.
3. **New evidence.** After round 1, S1 read more peer pages and found three overlaps the pack had missed (see "Evidence that changed the result"). Two new candidates (V4 and T4) were drafted to answer them.
4. **Blind review, round 2.** Three fresh reviewer agents scored all eleven candidates from the updated pack. The round 2 median decides; the author's score breaks nothing.

All six reviewers ran on 2026-11-02 and worked only from the pack. The pack's peer evidence is paraphrased in the next section.

## Evidence that changed the result

- **"Founders Helping Founders" is a registered trademark.** Founders Network's media kit calls it the official tagline and marks it with ® (https://foundersnetwork.com/media-kit/, read 2026-11-02). M2 contains it word for word, so M2 scores 0 on distinct.
- **Two peers already tell the "not alone" story.** Colorado Startup Week's origin story says its founders knew what building alone felt like. They started the week so others would not have to (https://www.costartupweek.com/, read 2026-11-02). Startup Colorado says it makes sure you are not starting up alone (https://startupcolorado.org/join-the-community/, read 2026-11-02). V1 shares that core claim, so it drops to 1 on distinct.
- **"Building together" appears in Colorado Startup Week's sponsor thanks** (same page). T3 shares the key phrase, so it drops to 1 on distinct.
- **"A community of founders" is the category's own descriptor.** Founders Network calls itself a mentoring community for tech founders (https://foundersnetwork.com/, read 2026-11-02). All six reviewers marked T1 down on distinct for this, and S1 agrees.

- **V2 lost** on three counts. It runs past 12 words. The outposts are on the Front Range, not in "any town". And Startup Colorado's mission already covers building where you call home.

## Tagline

| ID | Candidate | Words | Founders | Colorado | Short | True | Distinct | Author | Round 1 (A, B, C) | Round 2 (A, B, C) | Median |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **T4** | **Colorado founders, in good company.** | 5 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 10 | not in round 1 | 10, 9, 10 | **10** |
| T1 | A community of founders building companies in Colorado. (website decision 46) | 8 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 1 | 9 | 9, 9, 9 | 9, 9, 9 | 9 |
| T2 | Founders building Colorado. | 3 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 1 | 2 | 9 | 9, 9, 9 | 9, 10, 9 | 9 |
| T3 | Where Colorado founders build together. | 5 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 1 | 9 | 10, 10, 10 | 9, 9, 9 | 9 |

- **T4 wins, so the tagline changes.** The brief keeps decision 46's line unless the rubric shows a stronger founders-first line. T4 beats T1 by a point on the round 2 median. "In good company" carries two meanings at once: founders among good people, and founders among good companies (the Leaderboard). It says "you are not alone" without borrowing the peers' wording. One reviewer read "good company" as an evaluative claim. The other two, and S1, read it as shown on the site. Founders answer founders in the Wednesday thread, and every Leaderboard listing is sourced.
- **T1 stays as the descriptor.** It is the plain line that says what Colorado Startups is, for meta descriptions, directory listings and anywhere a reader needs a literal label. It lost as a tagline only because "a community of founders" is the whole category's descriptor.
- **T3 led round 1** with 10 from every reviewer, then dropped to 9 once the pack showed Colorado Startup Week's "building together" line.
- **T2 lost** on true today: founders here build companies, and "building Colorado" claims an outcome nobody can show.
- **Clearance.** No Colorado founder community uses "in good company" as a line (web search, 2026-11-02). The idiom is common in other trades, so S13's USPTO knockout search should cover the tagline with the mark.
- **Reverse.** Put T1 back as the tagline and drop T4. The change touches the website's footer, OG cards and tests (Wave 3, W2).

## Not scored by the brief

- **Purpose** is a belief, not a claim about Colorado Startups, so the rubric's "distinct" and "true today" do not apply. It rests on the Boulder Thesis and on a Kauffman study Brad Feld summarized (https://feld.com/archives/2014/04/empirical-support-boulder-thesis/, read 2026-11-02): demand for peer-based learning among entrepreneurs is high.
- **Promise** is the live homepage line, kept word for word so the site and the strategy say the same thing.
