20. **2026-11-02, Paul. Colors come from evidence, not preference.** Every main color in a palette carries a `rationale` that answers six points:
    1. **Role:** ground, ink, accent, link, status or data.
    2. **Distinctiveness:** where it sits against Colorado ecosystem peers, from the category color audit in `research/color/`. Before the audit lands, state the assumption.
    3. **Heritage equity:** the 2014 shield's teal and purple carry whatever recognition the brand has built. Keeping, evolving or retiring them, or any other hue, is a measured trade-off. Color alone is a weak distinctive asset next to shape (Phua et al., Intl. J. of Advertising, 2026-03-05), so recognition rests mainly on the mark.
    4. **Concept fit:** the Hall's forge world of gold, fire and light.
    5. **Access:** WCAG 2.2 AA (4.5:1 for text, 3:1 for large text and UI parts) and color-blind safety.
    6. **Cliché check:** avoid overused Colorado cues: brown, forest green, "Colorado blue", flag-C red, and gold used as a flag.

    Claims that a color means something ("blue means trust") count as weak evidence unless a study is cited. Teal stays or goes on this evidence. Reverse: Paul names colors by preference.
